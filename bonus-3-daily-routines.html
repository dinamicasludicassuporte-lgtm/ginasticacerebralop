How to Use This Bonus

1
Print the routines you need most and post them where the routine happens.
2
Check off each step as you go — the visible progress helps a lot.
3
It's okay to skip a step on a hard day. Read the tip for how to adapt.
4
Try to keep the order the same each time, even if the exact time shifts.
5
Routines work best with a calm, consistent tone — not as a strict checklist to enforce.

Morning Activity #1

Routine: Calm Morning Wake-Up

🕐 7:00 AM
Turn on a soft light and open the curtains
Take 3 slow, deep breaths together in bed
Stretch arms up high, then touch your toes
Say one thing you're looking forward to today
💡 Tip: If they wake up grumpy, skip straight to the stretch — movement helps mood more than talking does.

Morning Activity #2

Routine: Getting Dressed Without a Fight

🕐 7:15 AM
Lay out clothes the night before so there's no choosing in the morning
Set a 5-minute timer
Get dressed one item at a time, checking each one off
High-five when fully dressed
💡 Tip: If they refuse an item, offer 2 pre-approved choices instead of asking open-ended questions.

Morning Activity #3

Routine: Breakfast Time

🕐 7:30 AM
Sit down together, even for 5 minutes
Choose from the same 2–3 breakfast options each day
Take 5 bites minimum before leaving the table
Clear your own plate when done
💡 Tip: If they're not hungry, offer a smaller portion — pressure to finish often backfires.

Morning Activity #4

Routine: Leaving the House Smoothly

🕐 7:50 AM
Give a 10-minute warning, then a 5-minute warning
Shoes and bag by the door the night before
Do a 'final check': bag, shoes, jacket
Say a goodbye ritual (high-five, phrase, or hug)
💡 Tip: If they're still not ready at the warning, don't rush silently — narrate the plan out loud: 'shoes, then bag, then door.'

Morning Activity #5

Routine: Arriving at School Calmly

🕐 8:15 AM
Take one big breath together before getting out
Repeat a short calming phrase ('I've got this')
Walk in at your own pace, no rushing
End with the same goodbye every day
💡 Tip: If they cling or stall, keep the goodbye short and consistent — long goodbyes tend to increase anxiety.

After School Activity #6

Routine: After-School Decompression

🕐 3:30 PM
Give 15–20 minutes of no-talking downtime first
Offer a snack and water
Let them choose a quiet activity (no questions about school yet)
Ask about their day only after they seem settled
💡 Tip: If they melt down right after school, that's normal 'holding it together all day' fatigue — decompression comes before conversation.

After School Activity #7

Routine: Snack & Reset

🕐 3:45 PM
Offer 2 snack choices
Sit somewhere calm, away from screens
Take 5 minutes with no demands or questions
Do one big stretch before moving on
💡 Tip: If they want to skip straight to screens, that's okay occasionally — the goal is a reset, not a strict rule.

After School Activity #8

Routine: Homework Time

🕐 4:15 PM
Set a visible timer for a short work block (10–15 min)
Break work into 2–3 small chunks
Take a 2-minute movement break between chunks
Celebrate finishing, not perfection
💡 Tip: If they're stuck, don't add more time pressure — shrink the task instead ('just do 3 problems').

After School Activity #9

Routine: Screen Time Wind-Down

🕐 5:00 PM
Give a 10-minute and a 2-minute warning before screens end
Offer to help them save/pause their game
Transition straight into a physical activity
Avoid new screen requests until the next scheduled time
💡 Tip: If the warning triggers a meltdown, try a visual timer next time — seeing time pass is often easier than hearing it.

Before Bed Activity #10

Routine: Dinner Time Together

🕐 6:00 PM
Sit at the table together, screens off
Use the same seat each night for predictability
Share one 'high' and one 'low' from the day
Help clear the table when finished
💡 Tip: If they won't eat, don't turn it into a battle — offer one safe food alongside the family meal.

Anytime / Transitions Activity #16

Routine: Chore Time Made Easy

🕐 Anytime
Pick one small chore, not a whole list
Set a timer to make it a beatable challenge
Work alongside them for the first minute
Celebrate finishing right away, no delay
💡 Tip: If they refuse, shrink the chore further — 'just the socks' is easier to say yes to than 'clean your room.'

Anytime / Transitions Activity #17

Routine: Transition Warning Routine

🕐 As Needed
Give a 5-minute warning out loud
Give a 2-minute warning with a visual cue (show fingers or a timer)
Announce the transition clearly: 'Now it's time to...'
Offer a small choice about how the transition happens
💡 Tip: If a transition still causes a meltdown, that's information, not failure — try adding an extra warning step next time.

Anytime / Transitions Activity #18

Routine: Waiting Room / Car Ride Routine

🕐 As Needed
Bring one small quiet activity or fidget
Play a no-materials word game (see Bonus #2)
Take slow breaths together if it's taking a while
Praise the waiting, not just the final behavior
💡 Tip: If waiting is especially hard, tell them how long it will likely be using a comparison ('about as long as one show').

Anytime / Transitions Activity #19

Routine: Meltdown Reset Routine

🕐 As Needed
Lower your voice and get down to their level
Reduce the sensory noise around them if possible
Offer one simple choice (sit, walk, or squeeze a pillow)
Wait for calm before talking about what happened
💡 Tip: If nothing seems to help, check Bonus #4 (Emergency Calm-Down Activities) for faster in-the-moment techniques.

Anytime / Transitions Activity #20

Routine: New Place / New Situation Routine

🕐 As Needed
Preview what will happen before you arrive, in order
Point out one thing that will stay the same (a snack, a toy, a person)
Give them a job to do when you arrive (hold the bag, ring the bell)
Debrief afterward: what was easy, what was hard
💡 Tip: If anxiety builds before you even leave, try a photo or simple drawing of the new place ahead of time.
